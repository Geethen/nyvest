Blocked-CV Benchmark Report

Headline

Foundation models (run uncapped) take the top two spots. TabICL at the full training support reaches F1 0.687, beating the best tree ensemble (XGBoost, F1 0.653) by ~3.4 points and edging out TabPFN v3 (F1 0.680). Under the default 5 000-row cap both foundation models drop below the trees.

Class 12 (159 rows) is the headline per-class result. At 1 000 training rows TabICL scores F1 0.522 on this rare class; the classical models sit at 0.05–0.23. The classical gap closes as more data flows in (linear reaches 0.67 at full support); TabICL declines at full support (0.48). The pattern repeats more mildly for class 2 (600 rows) and class 10 (5 304 rows).
Class 1 (352 rows) is essentially unlearnable across all four models regardless of training-set size (F1 ≤ 0.07). The feature space (AlphaEarth + LiDAR) appears not to separate this class from its neighbours at the rates seen elsewhere — worth inspecting class-1 confusion matrices in a follow-up if class-1 recall is operationally important.
For mid-frequency classes (5–7) TabICL gives up its small-sample edge at full support. XGBoost takes the win on classes 5, 7, 8, 9, 12 at full data; TabICL retains the lead on classes 3, 4, 10, 11. No single model wins everywhere — an ensemble (or class-conditional model routing) could capture both regimes.

Key takeaways

  • Foundation models win when given the full support set. Both TabPFN v3 and TabICL beat the best tree ensemble by 3–3.5 F1 points at full per-fold train size; under the legacy 5 000-row cap they fall below trees.
  • TabICL is the recommended production candidate on this feature space — best F1, ~14× faster inference than TabPFN v3 at the same support, and lower peak memory (1.7 GB vs. 2.4 GB).
  • XGBoost remains the strongest "classical" option if foundation-model inference latency is unaffordable. CatBoost and the linear baseline are statistical ties with it on this CV — pick on speed/operational simplicity.
  • Spatial blocking lowers all scores vs. the random-split dryrun (XGBoost 0.697 → 0.653; tabpfn_v3 0.757 at full train, full val → 0.680 here). Blocked CV is the more honest estimate for tile-wide prediction.
  • LightGBM regression is a red flag. Under blocked CV it crashes to F1 0.476 (from 0.696 in the dryrun). Likely a class-balance / num_class interaction under the new fold partition — needs investigation before further use.
  • Cap policy: keep FOUNDATION_MAX_TRAIN at 5 000 only for sweep iterations; use uncapped foundation runs (especially TabICL) for the production scoring pass.
  • Learning curves favour the linear baseline at small support among classical models: with ≤ 20 k training rows the linear model is the top of the three classical contenders; trees only pull ahead at full per-fold support, and even then by ≤ 0.003 F1.
  • TabICL dominates the small-data regime across the board: at 1 000 rows it beats all three classical models on macro-F1 (0.614 vs 0.523–0.558) and is the per-class leader on 10 of 12 classes. Its peak is at 5 000 rows; at full support it shows fold-to-fold instability and gives up some per-class leads — particularly rare class 12, where it tops the small-data leaderboard (F1 0.52 at 1 k rows) but lags trees at full support.
  • Rare-class signal: class 12 (159 rows) is recoverable — TabICL @ 1 k or trees @ full support both push F1 from ~0.05 to 0.48–0.67. Class 1 (352 rows) is not — every model sits at F1 ≤ 0.07 regardless of training-set size, suggesting the feature space does not separate that class.
