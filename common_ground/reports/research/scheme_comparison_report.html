2. Findings and recommendation

What we learned from the scheme comparison

  • TabICL zero-shot wins every scheme. Scheme A: F1 = 0.6743, B: 0.6728, C: 0.6750. The relative advantage over CatBoost grows as the label space shrinks: +0.0039 (A), +0.0146 (B), +0.0133 (C). TabICL benefits more from cleaner labels.
  • Scheme A (1+2 → "bare") is the most defensible merger and posts the best absolute F1 of all three (0.6743). Both classes are Snaumark (Norwegian for "bare land") at different substrate types — ecologically the same umbrella. The performance gain is essentially "free" because class 1 (n=352, baseline F1=0.04) was effectively unusable in the 12-class setup.
  • Scheme B's extra water-merge (8+9 → "water") trades absolute F1 for fine-tune eligibility. F1 = 0.6728 (vs 0.6743 for A), but B has exactly 10 classes — the cap for TabICL fine-tuning. If lower-variance predictions from fine-tuning matter (we saw σ drop from 0.027 to 0.014 in round 4), Scheme B is the only way to enable that pathway without breaking ecological coherence further.
  • Scheme C (also merging 11+12) gives the lowest TabICL fold variance (σ = 0.020) and the best balanced accuracy (0.669). But the snow class (n=159) disappears, which is a real ecological loss if monitoring alpine surfaces is a downstream goal. Macro-F1 (0.6750) is essentially tied with Scheme A.
  • CatBoost high_iter is unstable under class merging. It loses to plain baseline in Scheme A (0.6605 vs 0.6704) because fold 2 collapsed (F1 = 0.6234). It recovers in B and C. This is the opposite of what we saw on the 12-class problem and suggests CatBoost's optimal iteration count is class-count dependent — a hyperparameter that should be re-tuned per scheme.
  • All three schemes beat the 12-class baseline (0.6197). Even the worst scheme/config combo (CatBoost high_iter on A, F1 = 0.6605) is +0.041 above the 12-class cg_llto. Some of this is mechanical (fewer classes = easier macro mean) and some is genuine signal (the rare classes 1 and 12 had near-zero F1 and were dragging the average down). The "easier macro" effect is bounded — removing class 1 entirely from the 12-class score would lift it to ~0.673, in line with Scheme A.

Recommendation

Use Scheme A (11 classes: 1+2 → "bare") with TabICL zero-shot, 10k support. Best macro-F1 (0.6743), preserves the freshwater/marine and sparse/snow distinctions that Schemes B and C sacrifice. The merger is ecologically grounded (both classes are Snaumark).

If you want fine-tuning's variance reduction: use Scheme B (10 classes) — it's the only scheme that fits TabICL's max_classes=10 ceiling without losing the snow class.

Watch-outs

  • The macro-F1 numbers across schemes are NOT directly comparable. Each scheme has a different number of classes — fewer classes mechanically inflates the macro-mean. Compare to the corresponding scheme's CatBoost reference, not to numbers from other schemes.
  • CatBoost iteration count should be re-tuned per scheme. 500 wins for Scheme A, 1 000 wins for B and C. If you commit to a scheme, sweep iterations again.
