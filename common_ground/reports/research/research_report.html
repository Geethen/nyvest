Misclassification patterns

Dominant confusion pairs from the aggregated cg_llto confusion matrix:

  • Class 1 → 6 (123 of 352): class 1 is almost always predicted as class 6. Only 8 correct predictions across all folds. Tiny support (352) and heavy spectral overlap with class 6 are the root cause. Class-weight experiments help recall but at precision cost.
  • Class 5 → 3 (1 921 of 4 739): by far the largest absolute confusion pair. Class 5 sits spectrally between classes 3 and 4. No experiment tested here fixed this — oversampling or a targeted pseudo-label filter are the most promising next steps.
  • Class 6 → 11 (1 612 of 12 771): second-largest pair. Classes 6 and 11 share spectral signatures; all experiments show similar confusion rates here.
  • Class 7 → 6 (1 230 of 6 538): class 7 systematically bleeds into class 6. Temporal variance features may help since class 7 (likely dynamic cover) changes more across years than class 6.

4. Findings and suggestions

What worked

  • high_iter (1 000 rounds) is the only macro-level winner — Δ = +0.0047 F1 over baseline. The gain is modest but consistent across all 3 folds and almost halves fold-to-fold variance (σ 0.0168 vs 0.0228). Classes 4, 8, 9, 11, 12 all improve; the main cost is slightly lower class-1 F1 (0.018 vs 0.038). Doubling iterations takes ~8 s per fold vs ~4 s — easily within budget.
  • lgbm_cw nearly matches baseline (Δ = −0.002) while running on CPU-style LightGBM. With a GPU-compiled LightGBM build it would likely be competitive and fit much faster than the 25 s observed. Worth revisiting once a GPU LightGBM wheel is available.
  • class_weight raises balanced-accuracy significantly (0.654 vs 0.612), meaning minority classes are recalled better on average — just at the cost of precision on majority classes, which pulls macro-F1 down slightly. If the downstream task penalises misses on rare classes equally, class_weight is the right operating point.
  • class_weight_conf boosts class-1 recall the most (mean F1 = 0.107 vs 0.038 baseline) — a 2.8× improvement — while also lifting class-5 (0.416 vs 0.364) and class-7 (0.595 vs 0.584), at the cost of macro-F1 (−0.010). Suitable when class-1 recall matters more than overall accuracy.

What did not work

  • LightGBM (default) collapses on fold 2 (F1 = 0.435) — the small unstable training set in fold 2 (3 168 rows) seems to trip the default hyperparameters. Without GPU it also takes 7× longer than CatBoost. Not recommended without hyperparameter tuning.
  • conf_weight (soft pseudo-label trust) hurts macro-F1 (−0.012). Weighting pseudo-labels by their top-1 probability concentrates gradient on high-confidence examples and effectively reduces the diversity signal from uncertain examples. The baseline (hard equal-weight pseudo-labels) works better.
  • Combining class_weight + conf_weight (class_weight_conf) does not add up: it improves minority-class recall vs baseline but underperforms relative to class_weight alone on macro-F1, suggesting the two weightings interact negatively.

Suggested next experiments (not yet tested)

  • Oversample minority classes 1 & 2 in Stage-1 training. SMOTE or random over-sampling of classes 1 (n=352) and 2 (n=600) before Stage-1 would give the pseudo-labeller better coverage of these rare classes, potentially improving pseudo-label quality downstream. Expected benefit: class-1 F1 > 0.10 reliably.
  • Two-stage class weights: high weight on classes 1,2,5 only. Instead of inverse-frequency weights across all 12 classes, apply a targeted 5× boost to classes 1, 2, 5 only. This avoids degrading the well-performing majority classes (3, 4, 6, 9) that currently provide the macro-F1 floor.
  • Pseudo-label temperature scaling / top-k filter for class 5. Class 5 is heavily confused with classes 3 and 4. A targeted filter — keep pseudo-labels for class 5 only when top-1 prob > 0.70 — would reduce label noise on this confusion boundary.
  • Feature engineering: temporal variance from unstable years. The unstable parquet has 9 years of AlphaEarth bands per point. Aggregating per-band mean/std across years 2017–2025 and appending to the stable feature vector would add temporal change signal, which might help separate class 5 (transitional) from classes 3 and 4.
  • high_iter = 1 000 + class_weight on weak classes only. The cleanest next experiment: run high_iter with targeted weights on {1, 2, 5} and check whether the macro-F1 gain from 1 000 rounds is preserved while minority recall improves.
  • Conformal RAPS at tighter α (0.05) for pseudo-labels. The current RAPS filter at α=0.10 keeps ~30% of pseudo-labels but showed no macro improvement. Tightening to α=0.05 further reduces quantity but raises quality; pair with a high-iter Stage 2 to compensate for the smaller augmented set.
