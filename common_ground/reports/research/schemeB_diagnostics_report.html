4. Findings & recommendations

Diagnosis — what's wrong with each weak class

Class 5 (grassland) — F1 = 0.40. THE bottleneck class.
  • 1 986 of 4 739 grassland test rows (42%) are predicted as crop (class 3) — by far the largest single confusion. Another 13% go to forest.
  • Train support is adequate (3 159 rows/fold, the 6th largest class).
  • Stage-1 pseudo-label quality is poor: mean top-1 confidence 0.547, with 44% of grassland pseudo-labels below 0.5 confidence. The pseudo-labels are themselves noisy guesses between grassland/crop/forest.
  • Learning curve: improves from 0.30 (1k) to 0.40 (25k), but the slope flattens after 10k. Verdict: PARTIALLY data-bound, but feature-overlap is the binding constraint. Pure AlphaEarth embeddings cannot reliably separate grassland from managed cropland at 10 m resolution — these classes are spectrally near-identical during the growing season.
Class 2 (bare, merged 1+2) — F1 = 0.53.
  • 200 of 952 bare test rows (21%) go to scrub (class 6). Smaller secondary confusions with crop (10%), built (10%), forest (6%).
  • Very small support: only 635 train rows/fold — the second-smallest class after snow. Test set is also tiny (317 rows/fold).
  • Worst pseudo-label quality of any class: mean conf 0.46, with 71% of pseudo-labels below 0.5 conf. Stage 1 cannot confidently identify bare pixels in the unstable set — because both source classes (sand and rock) are rare.
  • Learning curve: still rising at 25k (0.39 → 0.55, Δ = +0.07). Verdict: DATA-BOUND. This class would benefit substantially from more labelled training examples — especially of substrate variety (sand vs gravel vs exposed rock).
Class 7 (wetland) — F1 = 0.62. Closest to crossing 0.65.
  • 1 131 of 6 538 wetland test rows (17%) go to scrub (class 6). Another 11% go to forest. These are ecologically adjacent boundary classes (riparian zones, fen edges).
  • Train support is fine: 4 359 rows/fold.
  • Stage-1 pseudo-label quality is the second worst: mean conf 0.52, 49% low-conf.
  • Learning curve: rising up to 20k (0.59 → 0.62 → 0.62), modest gain through 25k. Verdict: partially DATA-BOUND for fold 1 (rises 0.553 → 0.719 from 1k to 25k), but feature overlap with scrub limits the ceiling.

Recommendations to improve weak classes

For class 5 (grassland) — feature-bound, hardest case

  • Add multi-temporal phenology features. Mean / std / range of NDVI or AlphaEarth bands across the growing season (May–Sep vs annual). Grassland and cropland have different phenological signatures — crops have one sharp peak, grasslands have a flatter, sustained growth curve. The unstable parquet has 9 years of data per cell; compute per-cell temporal statistics and append as features.
  • Bias the TabICL support set toward class 5. Currently random subsampling gives ~1 400/25 000 class-5 rows (5.6%). Force 4 000 (16%) by stratified sampling at the support level only. Not oversampling — just biased draw without replacement. This was previously tested at the 5 000-support scale where it hurt macro-F1 by ~0.01; retest at 25 000 where the macro-impact should be smaller.
  • Train a binary grassland-vs-crop expert. Two-stage routing: if TabICL predicts class 3 or 5, defer to a CatBoost binary classifier trained specifically on classes 3/5 with stronger emphasis on phenology features. Expected lift: 0.05–0.10 on class 5.

For class 2 (bare) — data-bound

  • Stratified support draw with class 2 boosted to 3× natural frequency. Currently only 39 pseudo-labels/fold for bare (because Stage 1 is uncertain). Draw stable bare rows preferentially: aim for ~3 000 (12%) of the 25k support. This trades some macro-F1 for major gain on class 2.
  • Improve Stage-1 confidence on bare pixels. The 71% low-conf rate suggests Stage 1 is the bottleneck for bare pseudo-labels. Either train Stage 1 with class weights (5× on class 2 specifically) or use a separate bare-detector model whose predictions are promoted to high confidence when above threshold.
  • Collect more labelled bare-ground points — this is the most direct fix for a data-bound class. Sand, gravel, and exposed rock should be sampled separately and then merged back into class 2.

For class 7 (wetland) — partially data-bound, partially feature-bound

  • Add hydrology features. Distance to nearest water body, slope (wetlands cluster on flat terrain), drainage density. These are derivable from DEM and existing hydrology layers and would directly address the 7→6 (scrub) confusion at riparian boundaries.
  • SAR/radar features (Sentinel-1). Wetlands have distinct backscatter signatures (volume vs surface scattering, seasonal flooding). The pure-optical AlphaEarth embeddings miss this. Adding a small set of Sentinel-1 VV/VH stats would target this specific confusion.
  • Stratified support boost (2× wetland). Less aggressive than class 2 since wetland support is already decent — try 2× natural frequency in the 25k draw.

Cross-cutting actions (help multiple weak classes)

  • Improve Stage-1 → better pseudo-labels. The current Stage-1 (CatBoost 500 iter) pseudo-labels are noisy for classes 2, 5, 7 (low conf, high entropy). Replacing Stage 1 with tabicl_25k_n16 (our current best) and using its predictions as pseudo-labels would propagate the same quality up the pipeline. Iterate Stage 2 again on the cleaner labels.
  • Per-class confidence-gated pseudo-labels. Currently all pseudo-labels go into Stage 2 regardless of confidence. Keep only pseudo-labels with top-1 conf ≥ 0.6 for the weak classes. This trades pseudo-label quantity for quality and could reduce label noise for grassland and wetland specifically.
  • Re-examine the wetland/scrub boundary in the labels themselves. 17% of wetland goes to scrub at test time. If grunnkart labels are themselves ambiguous at riparian edges, no model can resolve it. A sample audit of high-confidence wetland→scrub errors would tell us whether the model is wrong or the label is.

What we should NOT spend time on

  • Classes 4, 11, 8 are feature-bound and already strong (F1 0.72, 0.75, 0.95). More data won't help; only architecture changes (richer features or a better model class) would.
  • Class 12 (snow) gained from merging into nothing (we kept it separate) but is a tiny class (106 rows/fold). It now scores 0.72 in our best model — a non-issue.
  • Further n_estimators or support-size increases — we showed in round 3 that the curve has flattened at the data-axis.
